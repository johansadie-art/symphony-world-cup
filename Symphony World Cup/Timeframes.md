# Symphony AI World Cup — Timeframes

## Season Overview

A full season runs over one year — four quarterly rounds followed by the Grand Final Event, a standalone end-of-year celebration where the champion is crowned. Each round runs across a quarter, giving teams realistic time to build and test their solution alongside their day jobs. The Grand Final is not a round — no new solutions are built, and teams present the strongest ideas generated during the rounds.

| Quarter | Event |
|---|---|
| Q1 | Round 1 |
| Q2 | Round 2 |
| Q3 | Round 3 |
| Q4 | Round 4 |
| End of Year | Grand Final Event |

---

## Season Kickoff (Six Weeks Before Round 1)

| Timeframe | Activity |
|---|---|
| 6 weeks before Round 1 | Season announced company-wide. Problem Bank opens for department submissions. Region managers briefed on responsibilities and participation targets. |
| 4 weeks before Round 1 | Problem Bank closes for submissions. Leadership validates and scopes entries. Final Problem Bank published. |
| 2 weeks before Round 1 | Team registration opens. Matching pool opens for individuals without a team. Track B problem submissions accepted. |
| 1 week before Round 1 | Track B submissions reviewed. Approval panel responds within 48 hours. Team rosters finalised and submitted to region managers. All discipline gaps resolved. |
| Day 0 | Round 1 officially begins. |

---

## Quarterly Round Structure

Every round follows the same phased structure across approximately 10 to 12 weeks.

### Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–2)

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| Round begins. Teams locked. Problems confirmed. | No changes to team rosters after this point. |
| Process improvement teams begin as-is documentation, led by the SME. | Must be complete by end of Week 2. |
| Net new teams define the opportunity and establish how value will be measured. | SME leads this. Must be complete by end of Week 2. |
| Solution design begins. AI tooling selected. | Teams agree on what they are building and how success will be measured. |

**Key deadline:** Problem definition and baseline documentation complete by end of Week 2.

---

### Phase 2 — Build (Weeks 3–6)

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| Core solution built using AI. | AI does the heavy execution — teams direct, validate, and iterate. |
| First test run completed on real data or real workflows. | Must happen within Phase 2. |
| Mid-point check-in with region manager. | Lightweight — a Slack update or 10-minute call. What has been built, early results, any blockers. Not a formal review. |
| Iteration based on early results. | Solution refined and stabilised. |

**Key deadline:** Solution running on real data or workflows by end of Week 6.

---

### Phase 3 — Results (Weeks 7–9)

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| Solution continues running. | Real results being gathered and recorded. |
| SME validates results. | Results must be quantified and signed off before pitch preparation begins. |
| Pitch preparation begins. | Teams use AI to stress-test the business case and simulate Dragon questions. |
| New state documentation completed. | Describes how things now work with the AI solution in place. |

**Key deadline:** All deliverables substantially complete by end of Week 9.

---

### Phase 4 — Demo (Weeks 10–12)

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| Region manager dry-run pitch. | Region manager plays Devil's Dragon — challenges numbers, pokes at feasibility. Teams refine based on feedback. |
| Final polish. | Decks, live demos, and data dashboards prepared. |
| **Demo Day.** | All teams pitch to the Dragons. 10 minutes per team, 5 minutes Q&A. Pitch order by random draw. |
| Dragon feedback delivered to every team. | Written feedback to all teams within 48 hours of Demo Day. Round scores and winner announced. |

---

## Between Rounds

There is a two-week reset period between the end of one round's Demo Day and the start of the next round. This gives region managers time to confirm returning teams, onboard new teams, process Track B submissions, and resolve any roster changes before the next round begins.

---

## Grand Final Event

The Grand Final is held at the end of Q4 as a standalone event. No new solutions are built. Finalists refine and update the ideas and solutions they built during the rounds, with the latest results.

| Timeframe | Activity |
|---|---|
| 4 weeks before Grand Final | Round 4 complete. Top 3 ideas per round confirmed and announced (up to 12 finalist ideas total). |
| Weeks 1–2 of Grand Final prep | Finalist teams update their results data and refine their presentation. Coaching sessions with the central organising team available on request. |
| Week 3 of Grand Final prep | **Grand Final Event.** Company-wide audience, in-person where possible with a livestream for all employees. Final format set by the central owner based on finalist count. Full Dragon panel including at least one external guest. Symphony staff cast a live vote. Dragons deliberate live. Champion announced using Dragon scoring plus staff vote. |
| Within 2 weeks of Grand Final | Winning solution enters fast-track implementation. Season retrospective completed. Next season planning begins. |

## Time Commitment Per Person

The expected commitment is 2 to 3 hours per person per week during an active round. Across a 12-week quarter this totals approximately 24 to 36 hours per person — manageable alongside full-time responsibilities because AI handles the heavy execution work. The team's job is to direct it, validate results, and communicate the outcome clearly.

Leadership is expected to visibly protect this time. Participation in the AI World Cup is legitimate work, not an after-hours personal project. Region managers communicate this clearly at the start of each season.

| Phase | Weeks | Typical Activities | Est. Hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | 1–2 | Problem scoping, baseline documentation | 4–6 hrs |
| Build | 3–6 | Solution building, first test run, mid-point check-in | 8–12 hrs |
| Results | 7–9 | Results gathering, validation, pitch prep | 6–8 hrs |
| Demo | 10–12 | Dry-run, final polish, Demo Day | 6–8 hrs |
| **Total per round** | | | **24–34 hrs** |
