# Symphony AI World Cup — Timeframes

## Season Overview

A full season runs over one year — four rounds followed by the Grand Final Event, a standalone end-of-year celebration where the champion is crowned. Each round runs for approximately 10 to 12 weeks, giving squads realistic time to build and test their solution alongside their day jobs. The Grand Final is not a round — no new solutions are built.

| Round | Duration |
|---|---|
| Round 1 | approx. 10–12 weeks |
| Round 2 | approx. 10–12 weeks |
| Round 3 | approx. 10–12 weeks |
| Round 4 | approx. 10–12 weeks |
| Grand Final Event | End of year (target: April) |

---

## Season Kickoff (Six Weeks Before Round 1)

| Timeframe | Activity |
|---|---|
| 6 weeks before Round 1 | Season announced company-wide. Pitch Board opens for department submissions. Region managers briefed on responsibilities and participation targets. Squad Coaches recruited. |
| 4 weeks before Round 1 | Pitch Board closes for submissions. Leadership validates and scopes entries. Final Pitch Board published. |
| 2 weeks before Round 1 | Squad registration opens. Matching pool opens for individuals without a squad. Track B problem submissions accepted. |
| 1 week before Round 1 | Track B submissions reviewed. Approval panel responds within 48 hours. Squad rosters finalised and submitted to region managers. All discipline gaps resolved. Squad Coaches assigned. |
| Day 0 | Round 1 officially begins. |

---

## Round Structure

Every round follows the same phased structure across approximately 10 to 12 weeks.

### Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–2)

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| Round begins. Squads locked. Problems confirmed. | No changes to squad rosters after this point. |
| Process improvement squads begin as-is documentation, led by the SME. | Must be complete by end of Week 2. |
| Net new squads define the opportunity and establish how value will be measured. | SME leads this. Must be complete by end of Week 2. |
| Solution design begins. AI tooling selected. | Squads agree on what they are building and how success will be measured. |
| Squad Coach introductory session. | Coach meets the squad, reviews the problem brief, and helps sharpen the scope. |

**Key deadline:** Problem definition and baseline documentation complete by end of Week 2.

---

### Phase 2 — Build (Weeks 3–6)

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| Core solution built using AI. | AI does the heavy execution — squads direct, validate, and iterate. |
| First test run completed on real data or real workflows. | Must happen within Phase 2. |
| Mid-point check-in with region manager. | Lightweight — a Slack update or 10-minute call. What has been built, early results, any blockers. Not a formal review. |
| Iteration based on early results. | Solution refined and stabilised. |

**Key deadline:** Solution running on real data or workflows by end of Week 6.

---

### Phase 3 — Results (Weeks 7–9)

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| Solution continues running. | Real results being gathered and recorded. |
| SME validates results. | Results must be quantified and signed off before pitch preparation begins. |
| Pitch preparation begins. | Squads use AI to stress-test the business case and simulate Scout questions. |
| New state documentation completed. | Describes how things now work with the AI solution in place. |

**Key deadline:** All deliverables substantially complete by end of Week 9.

---

### Phase 4 — Match Prep (Weeks 10–12)

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| **The Friendly.** | Squad Coach runs a pre-match pressure test — challenges numbers, questions assumptions, asks the uncomfortable questions Scouts will ask in the room. Squads refine based on feedback. |
| Final polish. | Decks, live demos, and data dashboards prepared. |
| **Match Day.** | All squads pitch to the Scouts. 10 minutes per squad, 5 minutes Q&A. Pitch order by random draw. |
| Match Reports delivered to every squad. | Written feedback to all squads within 48 hours of Match Day. Round scores, top three solutions, and round winner announced. League Table updated. |

---

## Between Rounds

There is a two-week reset period between the end of one round's Match Day and the start of the next round. This gives region managers time to confirm returning squads, onboard new squads, process Track B submissions, assign Squad Coaches, and resolve any roster changes before the next round begins.

---

## Grand Final Event

The Grand Final is held at the end of the year as a standalone event (target: April). No new solutions are built. Finalist squads refine and update their qualifying solution with the latest results.

| Timeframe | Activity |
|---|---|
| 4 weeks before Grand Final | Round 4 complete. Top 3 solutions per round confirmed and announced publicly (up to 12 finalists total). League Table for the season published. |
| Weeks 1–2 of Grand Final prep | Finalist squads update their results data and refine their presentation. Coaching sessions with the central organising team available on request. |
| Week 3 of Grand Final prep | **Grand Final Event.** Company-wide audience, in-person where possible with a livestream for all employees. Full Scout panel including at least one external Scout. All finalists pitch before any deliberation. Symphony staff cast a live vote. Scouts deliberate live. Champion announced using combined Scout score and staff vote. |
| Within 2 weeks of Grand Final | Winning solution enters fast-track implementation. Season retrospective completed. Next season planning begins. |

## Time Commitment Per Person

The expected commitment is 2 to 3 hours per person per week during an active round. Across a 12-week round this totals approximately 24 to 34 hours per person — manageable alongside full-time responsibilities because AI handles the heavy execution work. The squad's job is to direct it, validate results, and communicate the outcome clearly.

Leadership is expected to visibly protect this time. Participation in the AI World Cup is legitimate work, not an after-hours personal project. Region managers communicate this clearly at the start of each season.

| Phase | Weeks | Typical Activities | Est. Hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | 1–2 | Problem scoping, baseline documentation, Coach intro | 4–6 hrs |
| Build | 3–6 | Solution building, first test run, mid-point check-in | 8–12 hrs |
| Results | 7–9 | Results gathering, validation, pitch prep | 6–8 hrs |
| Match Prep | 10–12 | The Friendly, final polish, Match Day | 6–8 hrs |
| **Total per round** | | | **24–34 hrs** |
