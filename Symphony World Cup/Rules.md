# Symphony AI World Cup — Rules

## 1. Eligibility

- The competition is open to all Symphony employees globally, regardless of role, level, or region.
- Participation is voluntary but actively encouraged by region managers.
- Employees may participate in more than one squad in the same round. This is particularly encouraged for disciplines with lower headcount across the company — such as Design — where the same person may be needed across multiple squads to satisfy the discipline requirement.
- Region managers may participate as squad members provided they have arranged cover for their organisational responsibilities during the round.

---

## 2. Squad Rules

### 2.1 Size
- Minimum 4 people. Maximum 5 people.

### 2.2 Required Disciplines
Every squad must include at least one person from each of the following:

- **Design**
- **Product**
- **Engineering or Data**
- **Business SME** — a domain expert from the area of the business the problem relates to

All four roles must be filled. A squad that cannot fill a discipline must use the regional matching pool before the round begins. Entering a round with fewer than four people or with a discipline unfilled is not permitted.

### 2.3 Substituting a Discipline with AI
If a squad has exhausted the matching pool and genuinely cannot fill one discipline, they may substitute that one role with AI. The following conditions apply:

- Only one discipline may ever be substituted. Not two.
- The squad must explicitly demonstrate during their pitch how AI covered the missing discipline's responsibilities.
- The Scouts will assess whether the substitution was adequate. A weak AI substitution will be reflected in the score.
- This exception must be declared to the region manager before the round begins, not on Match Day.

### 2.4 Cross-Regional Squads
Squads may be formed across regions. The squad declares a home region at registration. That squad counts toward the home region manager's participation numbers.

### 2.5 Squad Coach
Every squad is assigned a Squad Coach — a senior Symphony employee who is not a player on the squad. The Coach is responsible for running The Friendly before Match Day and is available as a sounding board throughout the round. Coaches are coordinated by the region manager and assigned before the round begins.

---

## 3. Problem Rules

### 3.1 The Pitch Board (Track A)
- Squads may select any available problem from the Pitch Board.
- Problems are first come, first served. Once a problem is claimed by a squad, it is removed from the available list for that round.
- The named SME attached to the problem automatically joins the squad. If that person is unavailable, the squad must either choose a different problem or source an alternative SME from the same business area, subject to approval.

### 3.2 Open Pitch (Track B)
- Squads wishing to bring their own problem must submit a written problem statement of no more than 200 words before the round registration deadline.
- The problem statement must include: the problem or opportunity description, the business area affected, why it matters to Symphony, and the name of the proposed SME.
- The approval panel will respond within 48 hours — approved, redirected, or declined with reasoning.
- A declined problem may be resubmitted with revisions or the squad may switch to Track A.

### 3.3 Problem Scope
- Problems must be solvable and testable within a single round on a part-time basis.
- Problems that require executive sign-off, significant budget, or months of data gathering before any solution can be tested are not eligible.
- Problems may relate to internal operations, client delivery, or commercial processes.
- Net new ideas are eligible — not every problem needs an existing process to improve. Squads pursuing a net new idea must still be able to demonstrate real value within the round.

---

## 4. AI Usage Rules

- AI must be used as a core, demonstrable part of every solution. It is not sufficient to mention AI in passing.
- Squads must be able to explain which AI tools were used, how they were used, and what they enabled that would not have been possible otherwise.
- There is no restriction on which AI tools or platforms squads may use.
- AI usage in pitch preparation is permitted and encouraged. This is not considered an unfair advantage — it is part of the spirit of the initiative.

---

## 5. Deliverable Rules

Three deliverables are mandatory for all squads. A fourth deliverable applies depending on the type of solution. A submission missing any mandatory element will be disqualified.

### 5.1 The Working Solution *(all squads)*
- Must be functional and deployed at the time of the pitch. Prototypes, mockups, and demonstrations of what the solution "would" do are not eligible.
- The solution must have run on real data or real workflows during the round.

### 5.2 Measured Results *(all squads)*
- Results must come from actual usage during the round. Projected or modelled results are not eligible.
- The SME must validate the results as genuine.
- Results must be quantified. Qualitative statements alone ("the squad found it much easier") do not meet the standard.

### 5.3 The New State *(all squads)*
- Must clearly describe how things now work with the AI solution in place.
- Must be clear enough that someone outside the squad could understand and operate it without the original squad present.
- For process improvement squads: describes the improved process. For net new squads: describes the new capability that now exists.

### 5.4a As-Is Process Documentation *(process improvement squads only)*
- Must describe the current process in enough detail that someone unfamiliar with it could understand how it works today.
- Must include: who performs the process, how long it takes, frequency, known pain points, and current cost or effort estimate.
- Must be signed off by the SME as an accurate representation of the current state.

### 5.4b Opportunity Definition *(net new idea squads only)*
- Must define the gap, pain point, or opportunity the squad identified and why it matters to the business.
- Must include evidence that the opportunity is genuine — not assumed.
- Must be signed off by the SME as an accurate representation of the business context.

---

## 6. Pitch Rules

- Each squad has 10 minutes to present, followed by 5 minutes of Q&A from the Scouts.
- All squad members must be present for the pitch. A squad member who cannot attend must notify the region manager at least 24 hours in advance.
- Pitch order is determined by random draw on Match Day.
- Squads may not exceed their time. At 10 minutes the presentation will be stopped and Q&A will begin.
- Visual aids, live demonstrations, and data dashboards are all permitted and encouraged.

---

## 7. Judging Rules

- Each Scout scores independently using the published scoring criteria.
- Scores are not shared between Scouts until all squads have pitched.
- In the event of a tie, the Scouts deliberate and cast a deciding vote. The Guest Scout has the casting vote in a deadlock.
- A Scout who has a direct personal relationship with a squad member must declare this before judging begins. The head of the initiative will determine whether recusal is required.
- Scout scores and Match Reports are final. Results are not subject to appeal.

---

## 8. Grand Final Event

- The Grand Final is a standalone end-of-year event, not a round. No new solutions are built.
- The **top three solutions from each round** automatically qualify. There are up to twelve places at the Grand Final Event.
- A squad may qualify multiple times if they rank in the top three in more than one round — each qualifying solution is treated as a separate Grand Final entry.
- Qualifying squads present the solution they qualified with, updated with any additional results gathered since.
- The Grand Final uses an extended pitch format — duration set by the central owner based on finalist count.
- The Scout panel is more senior than round panels and includes at least one external Scout.
- The audience is company-wide. All employees are encouraged to attend or watch via livestream and to cast their staff vote.
- The Grand Final scoring combines Scout scores and the Symphony staff vote. The exact weighting is published before the event.

---

## 9. The Prize

### Round Prize
- Each round has its own winner, awarded a round prize immediately following Match Day.
- All members of the round-winning squad receive the round prize.
- The round winner is announced company-wide.

### Grand Final Prize
- The Grand Final prize is awarded to the overall champion at the Grand Final Event.
- All members of the Grand Final winning squad receive the Grand Final prize.
- Within two weeks of the Grand Final, the winning solution enters a fast-track implementation process. The squad's SME leads adoption within their part of the business with support from Symphony leadership. Progress is reported back to the company.
- Symphony reserves the right to implement strong non-winning solutions from any round if the business case warrants it. This will always be communicated to the squad and credited to them publicly.

---

## 10. General Conduct

- All participants are expected to act with honesty and integrity. Fabricated results, misrepresented data, or plagiarised solutions will result in immediate disqualification.
- Squads may seek advice and feedback from colleagues outside the squad during the round. Building the solution using people who are not registered squad members is not permitted.
- All solutions built during the competition remain the intellectual property of Symphony.
- These rules may be updated between seasons. Changes will be communicated to all region managers before the start of a new season.
