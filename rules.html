Symphony AI World Cup — Rules

Official eligibility, team, problem, judging, and conduct rules. Reference document aligned with the programme overview.

1. Eligibility

  • The competition is open to all Symphony employees globally, regardless of role, level, or region.
  • Participation is voluntary but actively encouraged by region managers.
  • Employees may participate in more than one team in the same round. This is particularly encouraged for disciplines with lower headcount across the company — such as Design — where the same person may be needed across multiple teams to satisfy the discipline requirement.
  • Region managers may participate as team members provided they have arranged cover for their organisational responsibilities during the round.

2. Team Rules

2.1 Size

  • Minimum 4 people. Maximum 5 people.

2.2 Required Disciplines

Every team must include at least one person from each of the following:

  • Design
  • Product
  • Engineering or Data
  • Business SME — a domain expert from the area of the business the problem relates to

All four roles must be filled. A team that cannot fill a discipline must use the regional matching pool before the round begins. Entering a round with fewer than four people or with a discipline unfilled is not permitted.

2.3 Substituting a Discipline with AI

If a team has exhausted the matching pool and genuinely cannot fill one discipline, they may substitute that one role with AI. The following conditions apply:

  • Only one discipline may ever be substituted. Not two.
  • The team must explicitly demonstrate during their pitch how AI covered the missing discipline's responsibilities.
  • The judges will assess whether the substitution was adequate. A weak AI substitution will be reflected in the score.
  • This exception must be declared to the region manager before the round begins, not on demo day.

2.4 Cross-Regional Teams

  • Teams may be formed across regions. The team declares a home region at registration. That team counts toward the home region manager's participation numbers.

3. Problem Rules

3.1 Problem Bank (Track A)

  • Teams may select any available problem from the Problem Bank.
  • Problems are first come, first served. Once a problem is claimed by a team, it is removed from the available list for that round.
  • The named SME attached to the problem automatically joins the team. If that person is unavailable, the team must either choose a different problem or source an alternative SME from the same business area, subject to approval.

3.2 Open Pitch (Track B)

  • Teams wishing to bring their own problem must submit a written problem statement of no more than 200 words before the round registration deadline.
  • The problem statement must include: the problem or opportunity description, the business area affected, why it matters to Symphony, and the name of the proposed SME.
  • The approval panel will respond within 48 hours — approved, redirected, or declined with reasoning.
  • A declined problem may be resubmitted with revisions or the team may switch to Track A.

3.3 Problem Scope

  • Problems must be solvable and testable within a single quarterly round on a part-time basis.
  • Problems that require executive sign-off, significant budget, or months of data gathering before any solution can be tested are not eligible.
  • Problems may relate to internal operations, client delivery, or commercial processes.
  • Net new ideas are eligible — not every problem needs an existing process to improve. Teams pursuing a net new idea must still be able to demonstrate real value within the quarter.

4. AI Usage Rules

  • AI must be used as a core, demonstrable part of every solution. It is not sufficient to mention AI in passing.
  • Teams must be able to explain which AI tools were used, how they were used, and what they enabled that would not have been possible otherwise.
  • There is no restriction on which AI tools or platforms teams may use.
  • AI usage in pitch preparation is permitted and encouraged. This is not considered an unfair advantage — it is part of the spirit of the initiative.

5. Deliverable Rules

Three deliverables are mandatory for all teams. A fourth deliverable applies depending on the type of solution. A submission missing any mandatory element will be disqualified.

5.1 The Working Solution (all teams)

  • Must be functional and deployed at the time of the pitch. Prototypes, mockups, and demonstrations of what the solution "would" do are not eligible.
  • The solution must have run on real data or real workflows during the round.

5.2 Measured Results (all teams)

  • Results must come from actual usage during the round. Projected or modelled results are not eligible.
  • The SME must validate the results as genuine.
  • Results must be quantified. Qualitative statements alone ("the team found it much easier") do not meet the standard.

5.3 The New State (all teams)

  • Must clearly describe how things now work with the AI solution in place.
  • Must be clear enough that someone outside the team could understand and operate it without the original team present.
  • For process improvement teams: describes the improved process. For net new teams: describes the new capability that now exists.

5.4a As-Is Process Documentation (process improvement teams only)

  • Must describe the current process in enough detail that someone unfamiliar with it could understand how it works today.
  • Must include: who performs the process, how long it takes, frequency, known pain points, and current cost or effort estimate.
  • Must be signed off by the SME as an accurate representation of the current state.

5.4b Opportunity Definition (net new idea teams only)

  • Must define the gap, pain point, or opportunity the team identified and why it matters to the business.
  • Must include evidence that the opportunity is genuine — not assumed.
  • Must be signed off by the SME as an accurate representation of the business context.

6. Pitch Rules

  • Each team has 10 minutes to present, followed by 5 minutes of Q&A from the Dragons.
  • All team members must be present for the pitch. A team member who cannot attend must notify the region manager at least 24 hours in advance.
  • Pitch order is determined by random draw on demo day.
  • Teams may not exceed their time. At 10 minutes the presentation will be stopped and Q&A will begin.
  • Visual aids, live demonstrations, and data dashboards are all permitted and encouraged.

7. Judging Rules

  • Each Dragon scores independently using the published scoring criteria.
  • Scores are not shared between Dragons until all teams have pitched.
  • In the event of a tie, the Dragons deliberate and cast a deciding vote. The Wildcard Dragon has the casting vote in a deadlock.
  • A Dragon who has a direct personal relationship with a team member must declare this before judging begins. The head of the initiative will determine whether recusal is required.
  • Judge scores and feedback are final. Results are not subject to appeal.
  • For the Grand Final only, Symphony staff also vote for their favorite finalist idea.
  • The Grand Final winner is decided using Dragon scoring plus the Symphony staff vote.

8. Grand Final Event

  • The Grand Final is a standalone end-of-year event, not a round. No new solutions are built.
  • The top three ideas from each round qualify for the Grand Final (up to 12 finalist ideas in total).
  • Teams may continue entering later rounds with different problems, even after a round win.
  • Finalists present the idea and solution they built during the round, updated with any additional results gathered since.
  • The Grand Final compares ideas generated during the rounds. Teams are not expected to build anything new for the Final.
  • The Grand Final uses an event format set by the central owner and can be adjusted by finalist count.
  • The judging panel is more senior than round panels and includes at least one external Dragon.
  • The audience is company-wide. All employees are encouraged to attend or watch via livestream.
  • Symphony staff cast a live vote on their favorite finalist idea as part of the final decision.
  • The Grand Final scoring criteria differ from round criteria — see the main overview document.

9. The Prize

Round Prize

  • Each round has its own winner, awarded a round prize immediately following Demo Day.
  • All members of the round-winning team receive the round prize.
  • The round winner is announced company-wide.

Grand Final Prize

  • The Grand Final prize is awarded to the overall champion at the Grand Final Event.
  • All members of the Grand Final winning team receive the Grand Final prize.
  • Within two weeks of the Grand Final, the winning solution enters a fast-track implementation process. The team's SME leads adoption within their part of the business with support from Symphony leadership. Progress is reported back to the company.
  • Symphony reserves the right to implement strong non-winning solutions from any round if the business case warrants it. This will always be communicated to the team and credited to them publicly.

10. General Conduct

  • All participants are expected to act with honesty and integrity. Fabricated results, misrepresented data, or plagiarised solutions will result in immediate disqualification.
  • Teams may seek advice and feedback from colleagues outside the team during the round. Building the solution using people who are not registered team members is not permitted.
  • All solutions built during the competition remain the intellectual property of Symphony.
  • These rules may be updated between seasons. Changes will be communicated to all region managers before the start of a new season.
